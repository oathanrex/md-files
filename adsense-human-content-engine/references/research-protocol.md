# Research & Evidence Protocol

## 1. Research Trigger

Research when accuracy depends on information that may change over time.

Examples:

- prices
- laws
- regulations
- software versions
- product specifications
- company policies
- statistics
- current events
- rankings
- market information

For evergreen conceptual explanations, research may be unnecessary when the facts are stable and well-established.

---

## 2. Source Priority

Prefer:

1. Government and regulatory sources
2. Official company/product documentation
3. Academic institutions and peer-reviewed research
4. Professional organizations
5. High-quality journalism/reference sources
6. Community sources for community-experience questions

Use the lowest-level source only when higher-quality evidence is unavailable or when the task specifically concerns user experiences.

---

## 3. Claim Integrity

Treat every factual claim as one of:

### Verified

Directly supported by an appropriate source.

### Supported

Strongly supported by reliable evidence but not necessarily by a primary source.

### Contextual

A general explanation that does not require a precise external statistic.

### Unsupported

Cannot currently be substantiated.

Unsupported claims must be removed.

---

## 4. Numerical Claims

Numbers require special care.

Verify:

- percentages
- prices
- dates
- measurements
- statistics
- rates
- rankings
- financial calculations
- performance claims

Never transform an unsupported number into a vague estimate.

Do not use:

- "around 80%"
- "roughly 80%"
- "more than 80%"

when the underlying 80% claim is unsupported.

Remove it or replace it with a defensible conceptual explanation.

---

## 5. Source Representation

Never fabricate:

- URLs
- citations
- studies
- organizations
- experts
- quotations
- survey results
- statistics

Never attribute a claim to a source that does not support it.

---

## 6. Current Information

When current information matters and research tools are available:

Research before drafting.

Do not rely on outdated memory when the information can reasonably change.

---

## 7. Conflicting Sources

When credible sources disagree:

1. Identify the disagreement.
2. Prefer the more authoritative/primary source where appropriate.
3. Explain the relevant difference when useful.
4. Do not silently combine conflicting numbers.

---

## 8. Experience Claims

Never invent first-hand testing.

Valid:

"According to the manufacturer's specifications..."

"Independent benchmarks report..."

Invalid:

"I tested this..."

unless the user actually performed the test.

---

## 9. Research Stopping Rule

Do not research indefinitely.

Research until:

- the core claims are adequately supported
- important current facts are verified
- relevant practical constraints are understood
- additional sources stop materially improving the answer

Then draft.
