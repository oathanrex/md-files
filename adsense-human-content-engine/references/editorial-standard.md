# Editorial Standard

## 1. Reader First

Write for a person who wants an answer, not for a search engine.

The reader should understand the main point quickly.

---

## 2. Natural Voice

Use:

- active voice
- concrete language
- varied sentence length
- short paragraphs
- precise terminology
- conversational clarity

Use technical terminology when necessary, but explain unfamiliar terms.

---

## 3. Value Density

Every paragraph should materially advance understanding.

Useful paragraph functions include:

- answering
- explaining
- proving
- comparing
- qualifying
- demonstrating
- guiding

Remove paragraphs that merely repeat previous information.

---

## 4. Opening

Begin with the answer, finding, recommendation, calculation, or key context.

Avoid generic introductions such as:

- "In today's digital world..."
- "In this comprehensive guide..."
- "Whether you're a beginner..."
- "Let's dive into..."
- "In this article, we will..."

---

## 5. Transitions

Use transitions only when they improve logical flow.

Prefer direct connections between ideas over decorative transitions.

---

## 6. Conclusions

End when the reader has received the useful information.

Do not repeat the article merely to create a conclusion.

---

## 7. Formatting

### Tables

Use tables for genuine multi-variable comparisons.

### Bullets

Use bullets for:

- steps
- requirements
- options
- concise lists
- pros and cons

### Headings

Use headings to represent meaningful information groups.

Avoid heading spam.

---

## 8. SEO

Use important terminology naturally.

Optimize for:

- search intent
- topical completeness
- clarity
- useful internal structure

Do not optimize by repetition.

---

## 9. Readability

Prefer:

- one main idea per paragraph
- descriptive headings
- concise sentences where possible
- examples for complex concepts

Do not sacrifice precision merely to make writing shorter.

---

## 10. Human Quality

Do not intentionally insert:

- typos
- grammar errors
- random slang
- awkward phrasing

Human-quality writing comes from useful reasoning and natural expression, not artificial imperfections.

---

## 11. Forbidden Filler Patterns

Remove phrases when they add no meaning:

- "It is important to note that..."
- "As we all know..."
- "In conclusion..."
- "Final thoughts..."
- "Let's explore..."
- "Without further ado..."
- "In today's..."
- "When it comes to..."
- "Needless to say..."

The exact wording is not the issue; unnecessary filler is.
